Every posting this module has examined so far shares one basic-qualification shape: a degree, and a number of years, joined by "and" — both required, together. This lesson introduces two postings that break that shape, in opposite directions. The Sr. GNC Engineer posting opens the requirement into three separate branches, joined by "or," and one of those branches is the first of exactly two places anywhere in this role family where the module can point to professional experience formally substituting for a degree rather than merely supplementing it. The specialist postings — Navigation and State Estimation, and Operations Automation — go the other way, raising the formal floor to a graduate degree with no bachelor's-plus-experience alternative stated at all.

Neither of these is "the next rung" above Level I to II in any simple sense. They are two different moves on the same ladder, and understanding both, side by side, is the clearest way to see how differently a "basic qualification" can be constructed once a role stops being an entry-to-mid-level generalist posting.

## Sr. GNC Engineer: three ways to qualify

The stated basic qualification is:

> Bachelor's in computer science, aerospace, physics or engineering plus 5+ years of professional experience in GNC or software engineering, OR 7+ years of professional experience in lieu of a degree, OR a postgraduate degree in a related field.

This is a genuinely different sentence shape than anything in the previous three lessons: three branches joined by "or," any one of which is sufficient on its own. Meeting Level I to II's basic qualification required satisfying a degree clause and a years clause together. Meeting this one requires satisfying any single branch in full.

**Branch one** pairs a bachelor's degree — in computer science, aerospace, physics, or engineering — with 5+ years of professional experience in GNC or software engineering. Notice that this degree list restores computer science by name, alongside the same broad "engineering" category, closely matching the New Graduate posting's four named fields from two lessons ago. The ambiguity this module raised about a CS degree against the Level I to II posting's narrower "physics or an engineering discipline" phrasing does not carry over to this branch — computer science is named outright here.

**Branch two** is 7+ years of professional experience "in lieu of a degree" — no degree at all, of any field, provided the years threshold is met. This is one of exactly two places, anywhere in the postings this module covers, where a stated degree-optional path exists. Given how much weight a missing degree carries in every other posting this module has examined — a hard filter with no stated alternative at Level I to II and at New Graduate — this branch is worth taking seriously as a real, if long, route, and worth being able to state accurately without hesitation.

**Branch three** is a postgraduate degree — a master's or a doctorate — "in a related field." Read that phrase carefully, because it is genuinely different from the degree clauses in every earlier lesson of this module. New Graduate's degree clause named four fields explicitly. Level I to II's named two. Branch one of this very posting names four. Branch three, by contrast, uses exactly the kind of unenumerated "related field" language this module opened by warning you to read carefully, and this time there is no accompanying list to resolve it. The honest, non-speculative answer is that the module's source material does not enumerate what counts as "related" here, and inventing a confident boundary — asserting that a specific unusual postgraduate field definitely does or does not qualify — would be manufacturing certainty the text does not support. A postgraduate degree in a field with an obvious, direct connection to GNC work is a safe read of "related." A postgraduate degree in a field with a real but less direct connection is exactly the case worth asking a recruiter about explicitly, rather than guessing.

::: key
Sr. GNC Engineer — basic qualifications: bachelor's in computer science, aerospace, physics or engineering plus 5+ years of professional experience in GNC or software engineering, OR 7+ years of professional experience in lieu of a degree, OR a postgraduate degree in a related field.
:::

::: warning "A related field" in branch three is not a blank check
It is tempting to read branch three as though any graduate degree at all satisfies it, since no explicit list narrows it the way other clauses in this module do. The word "related" is still doing real work in the sentence — it is a qualifier, not a formality — and a graduate degree with no plausible technical connection to GNC work is not clearly covered merely because the clause does not spell out an exclusion list. Where the connection is not evident, treat the question as open rather than resolved in your favor.
:::

## What "senior" adds beyond a bigger number

None of the three branches above describe what changes about the actual work at this level — they only describe who is eligible to be considered for it. What changes about the work itself is stated separately: architecture and code reviews, writing production-quality code, leading mission-level design, and mentoring. This is not a longer list of the same kind of technical knowledge Level I to II already required — it is a description of ownership. A senior engineer is expected to review other people's architectural decisions and code, not only produce their own; to write code meant to last in production, not only to demonstrate a result once; to lead design at the level of an entire mission or vehicle program, not only a subsystem; and to mentor other engineers directly. The step from Level I to II to senior is, in a real sense, about ownership and judgment rather than about knowing more equations — a senior candidate is not expected to out-derive a Level I to II candidate on paper so much as to be trusted with decisions whose consequences reach further and whose correctness someone else is no longer double-checking as closely.

This has a direct, practical consequence for how a senior candidate should expect to be evaluated: the past-project deep-dive round, and the kind of behavioral evidence this module's next lesson examines in full, carries even more weight here than at Level I to II, precisely because ownership and judgment are demonstrated through specific decisions on specific past work, not through a credential.

## Specialist roles: when the floor becomes a graduate degree

Two named postings in this role family move in the opposite direction from Sr. GNC Engineer's third branch: rather than offering a graduate degree as one of several optional paths, they state it as the floor outright. Navigation and State Estimation, and Operations Automation — both named explicitly in this curriculum's earlier module on role families, the first doing star-tracker and signals-based attitude determination, inertial propagation, GNSS work and orbit determination, the second building the analysis tools, pipelines, and automation that support GNC work broadly — state their basic qualification as:

> A Master's or PhD in an engineering discipline, computer science or physics, plus experience developing, debugging and deploying software used in real-world applications.

Read this precisely rather than reading more into it than it says. The clause states the graduate degree plus relevant software experience as the qualification; it does not, in the text available to this module, explicitly rule out an alternative bachelor's-plus-experience path the way it might if it said so outright. What can be said accurately is narrower and still useful: no bachelor's-plus-experience alternative is named anywhere in this clause, in contrast to Sr. GNC Engineer's explicit second and third branches offering exactly that kind of alternative. For a candidate deciding whether to apply, the practical reading is the same either way — the stated floor is the graduate degree, and a bachelor's-only application would be relying on an alternative the posting never actually offers in writing.

Why a graduate degree sits as the stated floor here, and only here, is not something this module can state as a fact about this specific employer's reasoning — no such reasoning is given in the source material, and none should be invented. What can be said honestly is a general pattern visible across the industry: research-adjacent, specialist roles like these commonly set a graduate degree as the entry bar because the domain content itself — the theory behind state estimation, for instance, or the breadth of tooling and infrastructure work spanning an entire simulation and flight-software stack — overlaps heavily with material most engineers only reach through graduate coursework or research, rather than through an undergraduate curriculum alone. This is a typical pattern, not a universal law, and it is offered here as a plausible general explanation, not as a stated fact about why any particular posting is written the way it is.

::: key
Navigation and State Estimation, and Operations Automation — basic qualifications: a Master's or PhD in an engineering discipline, computer science or physics, plus experience developing, debugging and deploying software used in real-world applications.
:::

## Comparing the two moves

Put side by side, Sr. GNC Engineer and the specialist postings are doing opposite things to the same underlying idea. Sr. GNC Engineer adds a door: a candidate who cannot show a matching bachelor's degree still has two other stated paths available, one built on extra years and one built on a different kind of degree entirely. The specialist postings close a door: no bachelor's-plus-experience alternative is stated at all, and the graduate degree is not one option among several but the stated floor itself. Neither move makes one posting straightforwardly "harder" than the other in some single-dimensional sense — they are testing for different things. Seniority is a claim about accumulated ownership and judgment that years of experience, or a long professional record without a degree, can plausibly demonstrate. Specialist depth is a claim about a specific, deep theoretical or technical background that these postings treat as most reliably shown by exactly the kind of formal training a graduate program provides.

::: warning A specialist posting is not "one level above" Sr. GNC Engineer
It is easy to assume these two categories stack, since specialist roles ask for more formal education than even Sr. GNC Engineer's most degree-heavy branch requires. They do not stack. Specialist postings sit on a different axis entirely — depth of formal training in a specific sub-discipline — not a further rung on the same seniority ladder this module has been climbing lesson by lesson. A specialist candidate and a Sr. GNC Engineer candidate are shaped by genuinely different postings, not by the same posting at two different heights.
:::

::: example A near-miss on branch two, and why the shortfall matters less than the shortfall at Level I to II
A candidate has six and a half years of professional software engineering experience, much of it adjacent to GNC work, and no bachelor's degree at all. Checked against Sr. GNC Engineer's three branches: branch one fails immediately, since there is no degree to pair with the 5+ years. Branch two — 7+ years in lieu of a degree — is close but not yet met; six and a half years is short of the stated seven. Branch three fails, since there is no postgraduate degree either.

On paper, today, this candidate does not qualify for Sr. GNC Engineer. But notice what kind of gap this is: a specific number of months of continued professional experience on the same path already underway, not a structural mismatch. Contrast this with a candidate missing a bachelor's degree entirely while applying to Level I to II, a posting this module has already shown states no in-lieu-of-degree alternative at all — there, no amount of additional time closes the gap, because no branch exists to grow into. The same missing-degree fact pattern produces two entirely different situations depending on which specific posting states which specific structure.
:::

::: example A postgraduate degree in a field the text does not settle
A candidate holds a master's degree in applied mathematics, concentrated in optimization and statistical estimation, with two years of professional software experience since graduating — short of Sr. GNC Engineer's 5+ year branch-one threshold even if the degree qualified for it. Checked against branch three's "postgraduate degree in a related field": applied mathematics with an optimization and estimation concentration has an obvious, direct technical connection to GNC work, which makes "related" a plausible reading — though the posting's text does not confirm it explicitly, since mathematics was never named in this or any other degree clause this module has examined. Checked against the specialist postings' explicit list — "an engineering discipline, computer science or physics" — mathematics is not named there either, a genuine textual gap rather than an oversight this lesson can resolve on the candidate's behalf.

The honest position for this candidate is to treat both routes as plausible rather than confirmed, and to raise the question directly rather than assume an answer that happens to be convenient. Guessing wrong in either direction — over-confidently applying somewhere the degree clause was never going to cover, or wrongly ruling out a posting the field would likely have satisfied — costs real time either way.
:::
