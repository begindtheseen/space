Path 3 does not wait outside the target company's own filter and it does not enter through an adjacent function the way Path 2 does. It gets real, direct GNC experience somewhere else first — at a company whose hiring gate for that experience is looser — and lets the experience itself become the thing a later application counts, rather than the credential. This module names eight companies where that experience is genuinely available: Varda, Stoke, Firefly, Astra, True Anomaly, Impulse, Relativity, and Intuitive Machines.

## The companies, briefly and honestly

These are real, distinct companies pursuing different vehicles and missions, not a single interchangeable category. Varda builds reentry capsules for in-space manufacturing, with real atmospheric entry and recovery guidance work. Stoke is developing a fully reusable two-stage orbital launch vehicle, including a novel actively cooled reentry upper stage. Firefly builds the Alpha small launch vehicle and has flown lunar lander missions, spanning ascent guidance and lunar descent and landing work. Astra pursued small orbital launch, went through a well-documented difficult period including mission failures and a move to private ownership, and has since pivoted part of its business toward spacecraft propulsion — its trajectory has been rockier than the others named here, and that is worth knowing rather than glossing over. True Anomaly builds spacecraft for space domain awareness, with rendezvous and proximity operations as a central technical problem. Impulse builds in-space propulsion and orbital transfer vehicles. Relativity is developing the Terran R launch vehicle after retiring its earlier Terran 1 design. Intuitive Machines builds lunar landers and has flown commercial lunar descent and landing missions.

Every one of these companies' size, funding, and hiring needs changes over time, sometimes quickly, in a sector where that is normal rather than exceptional. Treat the list above as a starting point for research, not a verified current snapshot — this module's own resources point you to each company's own careers page and to community tracking resources such as the NASASpaceflight forum specifically because staying current matters more here than in most corners of this curriculum.

## Why the credential gate is looser here

A smaller, earlier-stage engineering organization typically runs a leaner recruiting function than an established, high-volume prime, without the same applicant numbers per opening that make a mechanical, resume-line filter necessary in the first place. A resume for a GNC-adjacent opening at a company with a few hundred employees is more likely to be read personally by an engineer or an engineering manager than processed by a large-scale screening system tuned to a literal degree-field match. In terms of the three-things distinction from this module's first lesson, this is a structural shift in how much weight the third layer — an individual's discretion — carries relative to the second — a mechanical filter — because the volume that forces mechanical filtering is not yet present. This is a general pattern across smaller, growth-stage technical organizations, not a documented policy at any specific company named here, and it varies company to company and even hire to hire.

::: warning "Looser" is relative to a large prime's process, not an absence of a bar
A NewSpace company hiring for GNC still wants evidence of real capability — the demonstrated work this curriculum builds matters here at least as much as anywhere else in this module, arguably more, since it is more likely to actually reach a technical decision-maker's eyes directly. "Looser gate" describes how formally the field-of-degree line is enforced, not how much the company cares whether you can do the work.
:::

## What the experience actually converts into

The module's own framing is precise and worth reading exactly as stated: two to three years at one of these companies converts into the professional GNC experience that Sr. GNC Engineer postings require, either as part of the 5+ years counted alongside a qualifying degree, or toward the 7+ years counted in lieu of one. Read "converts into" as accumulates toward, not as a fixed multiplier — two to three years at a smaller company is real, substantial, countable experience, and it is often disproportionately valuable per year, because a smaller team commonly hands each engineer broader scope across the guidance, estimation, and simulation stack than a narrower slice of a much larger program would. It is not, by itself, guaranteed to complete either the 5-year or the 7-year threshold on its own; how much of the total it supplies depends on what other experience you bring to it.

## Not only direct employment

The same logic — real GNC-adjacent experience, evaluated by people with more direct discretion than a large prime's queue, converting later into countable professional experience — often applies to contract engineering firms and hardware or software suppliers that serve these companies, not only to direct employment at the companies named above. This is a real, additional route worth knowing exists, though this module does not have specific companies to name in that category the way it does for the direct-hire ladder; if it fits your situation, research it with the same rigor this module's exercises ask you to apply to the named companies.

## What Path 3 costs, and what it does not solve

The years themselves are the most visible cost, and they are real years, not a shortcut measured in months. Less visible, and worth naming as honestly as the years: business risk. An early- or growth-stage aerospace company carries genuine risk of contraction, pivot, or failure that an established prime largely does not, and that risk is part of the honest price of this path, not a remote possibility to wave away. What Path 3 does not solve on its own is the field-of-degree question at a company with no in-lieu clause at all: most GNC-Engineer-titled roles, even at senior level, still want the bachelor's, or the 7-year in-lieu route, or the postgraduate route — Path 3 experience feeds the years side of that equation, and is exactly on point for the 7-year in-lieu branch specifically, but it is not a bypass of the field-of-degree question everywhere it appears.

Path 3 trades a looser, more personally evaluated hiring gate for real GNC experience, at real but genuine business risk. The companies named here — Varda, Stoke, Firefly, Astra, True Anomaly, Impulse, Relativity, and Intuitive Machines — change over time, and current status should always be checked directly.
:::

::: key

::: example Counting years toward two different thresholds
A candidate has 1 year of GNC-adjacent professional experience from an earlier role, then spends 2.5 years at a NewSpace launch company doing genuine guidance and simulation work, without a qualifying bachelor's degree.

Total professional GNC experience: $1 + 2.5 = 3.5$ years. Against Sr. GNC Engineer's 7+ years in-lieu-of-a-degree branch, the remaining gap is $7 - 3.5 = 3.5$ years — exactly half still to go. If this candidate instead completes an accredited qualifying degree through Path 1 at some point, the same 3.5 years of experience would count toward the 5+ years-with-degree branch instead, leaving $5 - 3.5 = 1.5$ years remaining — a materially shorter remaining distance, which is exactly why this module does not present the three paths as mutually exclusive alternatives chosen once and never revisited.
:::

::: example What survives a company's rough patch, and what does not
A candidate joins a small launch company's GNC team specifically for the broad, hands-on scope a larger company would not offer at the same career stage, and spends two genuine years working across guidance, simulation, and dispersion analysis. The company then goes through a significant contraction, and the role does not continue.

What did not survive: the specific job, and the assumption that this particular employer would still exist in its current form indefinitely — a real cost, and one the candidate did not control. What did survive, fully and portably: the two years of actual demonstrated GNC work, the specific technical judgment built handling real problems, and the professional relationships formed with people who now work in a wider set of places across the industry. The honest lesson is not that Path 3 failed this candidate — the experience is exactly as real and exactly as countable toward the next application as it would have been at a company that did not contract. It is that the risk named earlier in this lesson is a real cost of the path, felt at the level of the specific job, not at the level of the experience gained from it.
:::
