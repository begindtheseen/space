+++ practice | The average gap between touchpoints
--- task
A recruiter tells you how many touchpoints the process has and how many weeks it will take. Write a function `mean_gap_days(touchpoints, weeks)` that returns the average number of days between one touchpoint and the next, assuming they are spread evenly over the whole span.

- A week is 7 days. The first touchpoint starts the clock and the last one ends it.
- Return a float, for example `mean_gap_days(9, 4)` gives `3.5`.
- With fewer than two touchpoints there is no gap at all: raise `ValueError`.
--- starter
def mean_gap_days(touchpoints, weeks):
    pass
--- solution
def mean_gap_days(touchpoints, weeks):
    """Average days between consecutive touchpoints spread evenly over the span."""
    if touchpoints < 2:
        raise ValueError("at least two touchpoints are needed for a gap")
    return weeks * 7 / (touchpoints - 1)
--- hint
Count the gaps, not the touchpoints. Think of fence posts and the spaces between them.
--- hint
n touchpoints have n - 1 gaps. Divide the span in days by that.
--- check case | Seven touchpoints over six weeks
mean_gap_days(7, 6)
=> 7.0
--- check case | Nine touchpoints over four weeks
mean_gap_days(9, 4)
=> 3.5
--- check case | Two touchpoints: one gap, the whole span
mean_gap_days(2, 1)
=> 7.0
--- check test | One touchpoint, or none, raises ValueError
raises(ValueError, lambda: mean_gap_days(1, 4)) and raises(ValueError, lambda: mean_gap_days(0, 4))

+++ practice | When to start preparing
--- task
Your preparation needs `total_hours` hours, and you can give it at most `max_per_week` hours a week. The process itself lasts `process_weeks` weeks, and you prepare during it too. Write `start_weeks_before(total_hours, max_per_week, process_weeks)`: the smallest whole number of weeks before the recruiter's first call that you must start, so the work fits without any week going over the limit.

- Return `0` when the weeks of the process alone are enough.
- A part of a week counts as a whole week: 5.3 weeks of work need 6 weeks.
- `max_per_week` of zero or less raises `ValueError`.

For example, 80 hours at 10 hours a week need 8 weeks. A 4 week process leaves 4 weeks to do beforehand, so `start_weeks_before(80, 10, 4)` returns `4`.
--- starter
def start_weeks_before(total_hours, max_per_week, process_weeks):
    pass
--- solution
import math


def start_weeks_before(total_hours, max_per_week, process_weeks):
    """Whole weeks of preparation needed before the process starts."""
    if max_per_week <= 0:
        raise ValueError("max_per_week must be positive")
    weeks_needed = math.ceil(total_hours / max_per_week)
    return max(0, weeks_needed - process_weeks)
--- hint
First work out how many weeks the whole job needs at the weekly limit, rounding up. Then take away the weeks the process gives you.
--- hint
`math.ceil` rounds up. The answer cannot go below zero, so take the larger of zero and your difference.
--- check case | Two modules at 10 hours a week over a 4 week process
start_weeks_before(80, 10, 4)
=> 4
--- check case | A part week counts as a whole one
start_weeks_before(80, 15, 4)
=> 2
--- check case | The process alone is enough
start_weeks_before(40, 10, 6)
=> 0
--- check case | Exactly divisible hours do not round up
start_weeks_before(90, 10, 4)
=> 5
--- check test | Nothing to do, and a bad limit
start_weeks_before(0, 10, 4) == 0 and raises(ValueError, lambda: start_weeks_before(40, 0, 4))

--- task
After a round of take-homes, each candidate is a dictionary like `{"name": "Ada", "us_person": True, "score": 91}`. Write `advance(candidates, threshold)` that returns the names of the candidates who move on.

- A candidate moves on only if `us_person` is `True` **and** the score is at least `threshold`. The gate is pass or fail: no score, however high, makes up for failing it.
- Return the names with the highest score first. Candidates with equal scores keep their input order.
- No candidates, or nobody passing, gives `[]`.
--- starter
def advance(candidates, threshold):
    pass
--- solution
def advance(candidates, threshold):
    """Names that pass the eligibility gate and the score bar, best score first."""
    passing = [c for c in candidates if c["us_person"] and c["score"] >= threshold]
    passing.sort(key=lambda c: c["score"], reverse=True)
    return [c["name"] for c in passing]
--- hint
Filter first on both conditions, then sort what is left by score.

+++ practice | Gaps from real dates
--- task
Your calendar holds the dates of your touchpoints as text like `"2026-03-02"` (year, month, day). They may be out of order, and two touchpoints can fall on the same day. Write `gap_days(dates)` that returns the gaps between consecutive touchpoints in days, as a list of whole numbers in time order.

- Sort the dates by time first.
- Two touchpoints on the same day give a gap of `0`.
- Fewer than two dates give `[]`.
- Month ends and leap years must come out right, so work with real dates, not with the digits.
--- starter
def gap_days(dates):
    pass
--- solution
from datetime import date


def gap_days(dates):
    """Days between consecutive touchpoints, in time order."""
    days = sorted(date.fromisoformat(d) for d in dates)
    return [(b - a).days for a, b in zip(days, days[1:])]
--- hint
`datetime.date.fromisoformat` turns `"2026-03-02"` into a date. Subtracting two dates gives a gap whose `.days` is a whole number.
--- hint
Sort the dates, then pair each one with the next: `zip(days, days[1:])`.

+++ question | Why software roles are gated too
--- ask
Essentially all roles need US person status, including software and simulation roles that never touch a rocket. Why?
--- choice
Software engineers are often moved onto hardware teams later, so the company checks everyone up front.
--- choice correct
The controlled part is the technical data, not only the hardware. Showing it to someone who is not a US person counts as an export, even inside a US office.
--- choice
It is a company preference for citizens that the hiring team can relax for a strong candidate.
--- choice
The rule applies only in offices on government sites, and most engineering happens there.
--- why
Under ITAR, sharing controlled technical information with a person who may not receive it is an export, whether it happens by email, on a whiteboard or in a code review, and wherever the building is. Simulation and flight software engineers see exactly that information, so the gate reaches them too. It is a legal requirement, which is why the team cannot waive it for a strong candidate.

+++ practice | Cache lines a walk touches
--- task
A two-dimensional array of `rows` by `cols` elements, each `elem_bytes` bytes, is stored row-major starting at address 0: row 0 in full, then row 1, and so on. The processor fetches memory in cache lines of `line_bytes` bytes; the line an address falls in is `address // line_bytes`.

Write `line_changes(rows, cols, order, elem_bytes=8, line_bytes=64)`. It walks every element once, row by row when `order` is `"row"` and column by column when it is `"col"`, and returns how many accesses land on a different cache line from the access just before them. The very first access counts as one.

For example, a 4 by 16 array of doubles is 4 rows of 128 bytes. A row walk changes line 8 times; a column walk changes line on every one of its 64 accesses.

Any other `order` raises `ValueError`.
--- starter
def line_changes(rows, cols, order, elem_bytes=8, line_bytes=64):
    pass
--- solution
def line_changes(rows, cols, order, elem_bytes=8, line_bytes=64):
    """Accesses that land on a different cache line from the one before."""
    if order == "row":
        cells = ((i, j) for i in range(rows) for j in range(cols))
    elif order == "col":
        cells = ((i, j) for j in range(cols) for i in range(rows))
    else:
        raise ValueError(f"order must be 'row' or 'col', got {order!r}")
    changes = 0
    last = None
    for i, j in cells:
        line = (i * cols + j) * elem_bytes // line_bytes
        if line != last:
            changes += 1
            last = line
    return changes
--- hint
Element `(i, j)` of a row-major array sits at index `i * cols + j`, so its address is that index times `elem_bytes`.
--- hint
Generate the cells in the walk's order, compute each one's line, and count the times it differs from the previous line.
--- check case | 4 by 16 doubles, row walk
line_changes(4, 16, "row")
=> 8
--- check case | 4 by 16 doubles, column walk
line_changes(4, 16, "col")
=> 64
--- check case | Short rows share lines, even walking down a column
line_changes(4, 3, "col")
=> 6
--- check case | Two rows per line of 4-byte floats, column walk
line_changes(8, 8, "col", elem_bytes=4)
=> 32
--- check test | One element, and a bad order
line_changes(1, 1, "row") == 1 and raises(ValueError, lambda: line_changes(2, 2, "diagonal"))

+++ practice | Two threads, one tally
--- task
Two threads, A and B, each add one to a shared counter that starts at 0. Neither uses a lock, so each increment is three separate steps: **read** the counter into the thread's own register, **add** one to the register, **write** the register back to the counter. After a write, that thread's next step is a read again.

Write `run_schedule(schedule)`. The schedule is a string of the letters `A` and `B`; each letter runs the next step of that thread. Return the counter's final value. An increment whose write has not happened yet has not changed the counter.

For example, `"AAABBB"` runs the two increments one after the other and gives 2, while `"ABABAB"` has both threads read 0 and gives 1.

Any letter other than `A` or `B` raises `ValueError`.
--- starter
def run_schedule(schedule):
    pass
--- solution
def run_schedule(schedule):
    """Final counter after interleaving the read, add and write steps."""
    counter = 0
    register = {"A": 0, "B": 0}
    step = {"A": 0, "B": 0}
    for t in schedule:
        if t not in register:
            raise ValueError(f"unknown thread {t!r}")
        if step[t] == 0:
            register[t] = counter
        elif step[t] == 1:
            register[t] += 1
        else:
            counter = register[t]
        step[t] = (step[t] + 1) % 3
    return counter
--- hint
Keep, for each thread, its register and which of the three steps it will do next.
--- hint
Only the write step changes the shared counter, and it writes the thread's register, whatever the counter holds by then.
--- check case | One after the other
run_schedule("AAABBB")
=> 2
--- check case | Both read before either writes
run_schedule("ABABAB")
=> 1
--- check case | B finishes in the middle of A, and A overwrites it
run_schedule("AABBBA")
=> 1
--- check case | Three increments with no overlap, then an unfinished one
run_schedule("AAAAAABBBAB")
=> 3
--- check test | Nothing run, and a bad letter
run_schedule("") == 0 and raises(ValueError, lambda: run_schedule("ABX"))

+++ practice | A pool instead of new
--- task
A control loop must not allocate while it runs, so every buffer comes from a pool made once at startup. Write a class `Pool`:

- `Pool(capacity)` makes `capacity` slots, numbered from 0, all free. The pool never grows.
- `acquire()` hands out a free slot's number, or returns `None` when none is free. The slot released most recently comes back first; slots never used yet come out in increasing order.
- `release(i)` gives slot `i` back. A number outside the pool raises `IndexError`. Releasing a slot that is already free raises `ValueError`: that is a double delete.
- `free_count()` returns how many slots are free.
--- starter
class Pool:
    def __init__(self, capacity):
        pass
--- solution
class Pool:
    """A fixed set of slots handed out and returned, with no growth."""

    def __init__(self, capacity):
        self._in_use = [False] * capacity
        self._free = list(range(capacity - 1, -1, -1))  # a stack: slot 0 on top

    def acquire(self):
        if not self._free:
            return None
        i = self._free.pop()
        self._in_use[i] = True
        return i

    def release(self, i):
        if not 0 <= i < len(self._in_use):
            raise IndexError(f"slot {i} is not in the pool")
        if not self._in_use[i]:
            raise ValueError(f"slot {i} is already free: double release")
        self._in_use[i] = False
        self._free.append(i)

    def free_count(self):
        return len(self._free)
--- hint
A list used as a stack gives "most recently released first" for free: push on release, pop on acquire.
--- hint
Keep a separate list of booleans that says which slots are in use, so a double release can be caught.
--- check case | Hands out every slot, then nothing
(lambda p: [p.acquire() for _ in range(4)])(Pool(3))
=> [0, 1, 2, None]
--- check case | The slot released last comes back first
(lambda p: (p.acquire(), p.acquire(), p.acquire(), p.release(0), p.release(2), p.acquire(), p.acquire(), p.free_count())[5:])(Pool(3))
=> (2, 0, 0)
--- check test | Releasing a free slot is a double release
(lambda p: (p.acquire(), p.release(0), raises(ValueError, lambda: p.release(0)))[2])(Pool(2))
--- check test | A slot never handed out is already free
raises(ValueError, lambda: Pool(2).release(1))
--- check test | A slot outside the pool
raises(IndexError, lambda: Pool(2).release(5)) and raises(IndexError, lambda: Pool(2).release(-1))
--- check case | An empty pool
(lambda p: (p.acquire(), p.free_count()))(Pool(0))
=> (None, 0)

+++ practice | The overflow check that never fires
--- task
Someone ported a C++ overflow check to a Python model of 32-bit signed integers. `add_i32(a, b)` should return `a + b` when the true sum fits in a 32-bit signed integer (from minus 2 to the power 31 up to 2 to the power 31 minus 1), and raise `OverflowError` when it does not.

Instead it raises on ordinary sums like `5 + -3`, and it never catches a sum that really is too big. Fix it.
--- starter
INT_MIN, INT_MAX = -2**31, 2**31 - 1


def add_i32(a, b):
    s = a + b
    if s < a:  # from C: "if the sum went down, it wrapped"
        raise OverflowError("int32 overflow")
    return s
--- solution
INT_MIN, INT_MAX = -2**31, 2**31 - 1


def add_i32(a, b):
    """a + b, or OverflowError if the true sum does not fit in 32 signed bits."""
    s = a + b
    if not INT_MIN <= s <= INT_MAX:
        raise OverflowError("int32 overflow")
    return s
--- hint
`s < a` is true whenever `b` is negative. And a Python integer never wraps, so a huge sum is never smaller than `a`.
--- hint
Check the true sum against the two limits directly.

--- check test | One past the bottom overflows

+++ question | A move from a const object
--- ask
What does this program print?

```cpp
#include <cstdio>
#include <utility>

struct Frame {
    Frame() = default;
    Frame(const Frame&) { std::puts("copy"); }
    Frame(Frame&&) noexcept { std::puts("move"); }
};

int main() {
    const Frame a{};
    Frame b = std::move(a);
    Frame c = std::move(b);
}
```

Type the two words it prints, in order, separated by a space.
--- answer
copy move
copy, move
copy then move
copy and move
--- why
`std::move` is only a cast to an rvalue reference. On the `const` object `a` it gives a `const Frame&&`, which the move constructor cannot take, because moving needs to change the source. So the copy constructor is chosen, silently: "copy". `b` is not const, so `std::move(b)` really does pick the move constructor: "move".

+++ question | The size of an object with virtual functions
--- ask
On a typical 64-bit Linux build, where a pointer is 8 bytes and a `double` is 8 bytes, what is `sizeof(Wheel)`?

```cpp
struct Wheel {
    virtual ~Wheel() = default;
    virtual double torque() const { return 0.0; }
    double speed;
};
```

Type the number of bytes.
--- answer
16
16 bytes
--- why
In the scheme every major compiler uses, each object of a class with virtual functions carries one hidden pointer, the vptr, to its class's table. That is 8 bytes however many virtual functions there are, because the table itself is shared by the whole class. Add the 8-byte `double` and the object is 16 bytes. (The layout is implementation-defined; this is the common scheme to describe.)

+++ practice | Which index was read
--- task
An AddressSanitizer report says where a bad access landed, relative to a heap block, in a line like:

```
0x503000000060 is located 0 bytes after 32-byte region [0x503000000040,0x503000000060)
```

The phrase can be `after`, `inside of` or `before`. Write `bad_index(line, elem_bytes)` that returns the array index the code touched, for an array of elements of `elem_bytes` bytes that starts at the region's first byte.

- `located N bytes after M-byte region`: the access is at byte offset M + N.
- `located N bytes inside of M-byte region`: it is at byte offset N.
- `located N bytes before M-byte region`: it is at byte offset minus N.
- The index is the byte offset divided by `elem_bytes`, rounded down. A byte offset of minus 8 with 8-byte elements is index `-1`.
- A line with none of these shapes raises `ValueError`.
--- starter
def bad_index(line, elem_bytes):
    pass
--- solution
import re

PATTERN = re.compile(r"located (\d+) bytes (after|inside of|before) (\d+)-byte region")


def bad_index(line, elem_bytes):
    """The array index an ASan location line points at."""
    m = PATTERN.search(line)
    if not m:
        raise ValueError(f"not an ASan location line: {line!r}")
    n, where, size = int(m.group(1)), m.group(2), int(m.group(3))
    if where == "after":
        offset = size + n
    elif where == "inside of":
        offset = n
    else:
        offset = -n
    return offset // elem_bytes
--- hint
A regular expression with three groups can pull out N, the word, and M in one go.
--- hint
Turn each shape into a byte offset from the start of the block, then use floor division by the element size.
--- check case | Just past the end of ten doubles
bad_index("0x502000000090 is located 0 bytes after 80-byte region [0x502000000040,0x502000000090)", 8)
=> 10
--- check case | Four bytes past the end of ten ints
bad_index("0x60200000003c is located 4 bytes after 40-byte region [0x602000000010,0x602000000038)", 4)
=> 11
--- check case | Inside a freed block of two ints
bad_index("0x502000000014 is located 4 bytes inside of 8-byte region [0x502000000010,0x502000000018)", 4)
=> 1
--- check case | Just before the start
bad_index("0x503000000038 is located 8 bytes before 64-byte region [0x503000000040,0x503000000080)", 8)
=> -1
--- check test | A line that is not a location raises ValueError

+++ practice | Bisect the crash
--- task
A test run of `n` cases crashes somewhere. `crashes(k)` runs the first `k` cases (k from 1 to n) and returns `True` if that run crashed. Once a run of k cases crashes, every longer run crashes too.

Write `first_crashing_case(n, crashes)` that returns the 0-based index of the case that makes the run crash: the smallest `k` for which `crashes(k)` is true, minus one. If even the full run does not crash, return `None`.

Each call to `crashes` is a slow test run, so call it at most `ceil(log2(n)) + 1` times: 12 times for 2000 cases.
--- starter
def first_crashing_case(n, crashes):
    pass
--- solution
def first_crashing_case(n, crashes):
    """Index of the first case that makes the run crash, by bisection."""
    if not crashes(n):
        return None
    lo, hi = 1, n  # crashes(hi) is known to be true
    while lo < hi:
        mid = (lo + hi) // 2
        if crashes(mid):
            hi = mid
        else:
            lo = mid + 1
    return lo - 1
--- hint
Check the full run once. If it crashes, the answer lies between 1 and n, and each test can halve that range.
--- hint
Keep two ends, `lo` and `hi`, where `crashes(hi)` is known to be true. Test the middle and move one end to it.

=> (1336, True)

--- check case | Only the last case crashes

+++ practice | A lookup that adds nothing
--- task
`channels` maps a channel id to its scale factor. `readings` is a list of `(channel_id, raw)` pairs. Write `scale_readings(channels, readings)` that returns a list with `raw * scale` for each reading, in order, or `None` for a reading whose channel is not known.

- Looking up an unknown id must never add it to `channels`. The caller may pass a `collections.defaultdict`, which adds a key whenever you read it with square brackets.
- A known channel whose scale is `0.0` gives `0.0`, not `None`.
--- starter
def scale_readings(channels, readings):
    pass
--- solution
def scale_readings(channels, readings):
    """raw * scale per reading, or None for an unknown channel; never inserts."""
    out = []
    for channel_id, raw in readings:
        if channel_id in channels:
            out.append(raw * channels[channel_id])
        else:
            out.append(None)
    return out
--- hint
This is the `std::map` trap in Python: `d[key]` on a defaultdict inserts a default value, just as `operator[]` does on a map.
--- hint
Test membership with `in` first, which never inserts. Do not test the scale's truth value, because `0.0` is false.
--- check case | Known and unknown channels
scale_readings({4: 0.01, 7: 2.0}, [(4, 250.0), (5, 250.0), (7, 3.0)])
=> [2.5, None, 6.0]

(lambda d: (scale_readings(d, [(4, 250.0), (9, 1.0)]), sorted(d)))(__import__("collections").defaultdict(float, {4: 0.01}))

from math import comb


def p_at_least(k, n, p):
    """Chance that at least k of n independent units fail, each with chance p."""
    if not 0.0 <= p <= 1.0:

def mcdc_shown(decision, n, tests):
    """For each condition, whether some pair of tests shows it alone changes the outcome."""
    vectors = [tuple(bool(x) for x in t) for t in tests]
    outcome = {v: bool(decision(*v)) for v in vectors}
    shown = []

        for a in vectors:

            if b in outcome and outcome[a] != outcome[b]:
                found = True
                break
        shown.append(found)
    return shown

from itertools import combinations, product


def shows_all(decision, n, tests):
    """True if every condition has a pair in tests that differs only there and flips the outcome."""
    have = set(tests)
    for i in range(n):
        if not any(
            (a[:i] + (not a[i],) + a[i + 1:]) in have
            and bool(decision(*a)) != bool(decision(*(a[:i] + (not a[i],) + a[i + 1:])))
            for a in tests
        ):
            return False
    return True


def mcdc_minimum(decision, n):
    """Size of the smallest MC/DC test set, or None if a condition is masked."""
    rows = list(product([False, True], repeat=n))
    if not shows_all(decision, n, rows):
        return None
    for size in range(2, len(rows) + 1):
        if any(shows_all(decision, n, subset) for subset in combinations(rows, size)):
            return size
    return None
--- hint
First check whether the full set of all vectors shows every condition. If it does not, no smaller set can, so return `None` at once.
--- hint
`itertools.product([False, True], repeat=n)` lists the vectors and `itertools.combinations(rows, size)` lists the sets of each size. Return the first size that works.

def pd_design(J, wn, zeta):
    """PD gains, settling time and overshoot for a chosen wn and zeta."""

import math


def one_sig(x):
    """x rounded to one significant figure."""
    if x == 0:
        return 0
    return round(x, -int(math.floor(math.log10(abs(x)))))


def fermi_range(factors):
    """Low, high and geometric-mean estimate of a product of uncertain factors."""
    if not factors:
        raise ValueError("no factors")
    low = high = 1.0
    for lo, hi in factors:
        if lo <= 0 or hi < lo:
            raise ValueError(f"bad factor ({lo}, {hi})")
        low *= lo
        high *= hi
    estimate = one_sig(math.sqrt(low * high))
    return (float(f"{low:.3g}"), float(f"{high:.3g}"), estimate)
--- hint
Multiply the lows together and the highs together in one loop, checking each pair as you go.
--- hint

--- solution
import math


def runs_needed(p, confidence=0.95):
    """Smallest N with (1 - p)**N <= 1 - confidence."""
    if not 0 < p <= 1 or not 0 < confidence < 1:
        raise ValueError("need 0 < p <= 1 and 0 < confidence < 1")
    if p == 1:
        return 1
    alpha = 1 - confidence
    n = max(1, math.ceil(math.log(alpha) / math.log(1 - p)))
    while n > 1 and (1 - p) ** (n - 1) <= alpha:  # guard against rounding in the logs
        n -= 1
    while (1 - p) ** n > alpha:
        n += 1
    return n
--- hint
Take logarithms of `(1 - p)**N <= alpha`: `N * log(1 - p) <= log(alpha)`. Both logarithms are negative, so dividing flips the inequality.
--- hint

+++ practice | Splitting the word budget
--- task
A STAR story of `total_words` words is split across Situation, Task, Action and Result by whole-number percentages, by default 15, 10, 50 and 25. Write `star_split(total_words, percents=(15, 10, 50, 25))` that returns a dictionary with the keys `"situation"`, `"task"`, `"action"` and `"result"`, whose whole-number values add up to exactly `total_words`.

- First give each part its exact share rounded down: `total_words * percent // 100`.
- Then hand out the words still left over, one at a time, to the parts whose exact share had the largest remainder. When remainders tie, the order of preference is Action, Result, Situation, Task.
- Percentages that do not add up to 100 raise `ValueError`.

For example, 230 words gives 34, 23, 115 and 57, with one word left; Situation and Result tie on the remainder, and Result wins the tie, so it gets 58.
--- starter

    pass
--- solution
PARTS = ["situation", "task", "action", "result"]
TIE_ORDER = {"action": 0, "result": 1, "situation": 2, "task": 3}


def star_split(total_words, percents=(15, 10, 50, 25)):
    """Whole words per STAR part, adding up to total_words exactly."""
    if sum(percents) != 100:
        raise ValueError("percentages must add up to 100")
    exact = {p: total_words * pct for p, pct in zip(PARTS, percents)}
    split = {p: e // 100 for p, e in exact.items()}
    left = total_words - sum(split.values())
    order = sorted(PARTS, key=lambda p: (-(exact[p] % 100), TIE_ORDER[p]))
    for p in order[:left]:
        split[p] += 1
    return split
--- hint
Work in whole numbers: `total_words * percent` is the exact share times 100, so `// 100` rounds it down and `% 100` is its remainder.
--- hint

--- task
Write `sanitize(events)`, a small model of what AddressSanitizer and its leak check report. `events` is a list of tuples, run in order:

- `("new", name, nbytes)` allocates a block of `nbytes` bytes under `name`. A name may be used again after its block is deleted; that is a fresh block.

- `("read", name, offset, size)` and `("write", name, offset, size)` touch `size` bytes starting at byte `offset` of the block.

- deleting or touching a name whose block was deleted and not allocated again: `"double delete of NAME"` for a delete, `"use after free of NAME"` for a read or write;
- deleting or touching a name never allocated: `"unknown block NAME"`;
- a read or write with `offset` below 0 or `offset + size` beyond the block's size: `"overflow on NAME at OFFSET"`.

--- starter

    pass
--- solution
def sanitize(events):
    """The first memory error in the events, or the leak summary at the end."""
    live = {}  # name -> size
    freed = set()
    for event in events:
        kind, name = event[0], event[1]
        if kind == "new":
            live[name] = event[2]
            freed.discard(name)
            continue
        if name not in live:
            if name in freed:
                return f"double delete of {name}" if kind == "delete" else f"use after free of {name}"
            return f"unknown block {name}"
        if kind == "delete":
            del live[name]
            freed.add(name)
        else:
            offset, size = event[2], event[3]
            if offset < 0 or offset + size > live[name]:
                return f"overflow on {name} at {offset}"
    if live:
        return f"leak: {sum(live.values())} bytes in {len(live)} blocks"
    return "clean"

+++ problem | Row walk, column walk, and a real cache
--- task
A two-dimensional array of `rows` by `cols` elements of `elem_bytes` bytes is stored row-major from address 0. It is walked once, every element, row by row (`order` `"row"`) or column by column (`"col"`). The processor has a cache that holds `cache_lines` lines of `line_bytes` bytes, starts empty, and when full throws out the line used least recently. An access to a line not in the cache is a miss; the line is then brought in.

Write `cache_misses(rows, cols, order, cache_lines, elem_bytes=8, line_bytes=64)` that returns the number of misses. The line an address falls in is `address // line_bytes`. A `cache_lines` below 1, or an unknown `order`, raises `ValueError`.

For example, walking a 64 by 64 array of doubles column by column misses 512 times with a 64-line cache, but 4096 times, on every access, with a 63-line cache.
--- starter
def cache_misses(rows, cols, order, cache_lines, elem_bytes=8, line_bytes=64):
    pass
--- solution
from collections import OrderedDict


def cache_misses(rows, cols, order, cache_lines, elem_bytes=8, line_bytes=64):
    """Misses of a least-recently-used cache while walking a row-major array."""
    if cache_lines < 1:
        raise ValueError("the cache needs at least one line")
    if order == "row":
        cells = ((i, j) for i in range(rows) for j in range(cols))
    elif order == "col":
        cells = ((i, j) for j in range(cols) for i in range(rows))
    else:
        raise ValueError(f"unknown order {order!r}")
    cache = OrderedDict()
    misses = 0
    for i, j in cells:
        line = (i * cols + j) * elem_bytes // line_bytes
        if line in cache:
            cache.move_to_end(line)
        else:
            misses += 1
            cache[line] = True
            if len(cache) > cache_lines:
                cache.popitem(last=False)
    return misses

import struct


def decode_all(frames, schemas):
    """Decode each frame with its own schema version; count and set aside the rest."""
    rows, unknown, bad = [], [], []
    seen = set()
    duplicates = 0
    for sat, seq, version, payload in frames:
        if (sat, seq) in seen:
            duplicates += 1
            continue
        fields = schemas.get(version)
        if fields is None:
            unknown.append((sat, seq))
            continue
        if len(payload) != sum(struct.calcsize(fmt) for _, fmt, _ in fields):
            bad.append((sat, seq))
            continue
        row = {"sat": sat, "seq": seq, "schema": version}
        offset = 0
        for channel, fmt, scale in fields:
            (raw,) = struct.unpack_from(fmt, payload, offset)
            row[channel] = round(raw * scale, 6)
            offset += struct.calcsize(fmt)
        rows.append(row)
        seen.add((sat, seq))
    return {"rows": rows, "duplicates": duplicates, "unknown_schema": unknown, "bad_length": bad}

import numpy as np


def fly(rng):
    """One dispersed case: the peak load in kN."""
    thrust = rng.normal(1.0, 0.02)
    gust = rng.normal(0.0, 1.0)
    return 120.0 * thrust + 8.0 * abs(gust)


def rerun(base_seed, k):
    """Case k alone, with the seed derived from the base seed and the case number."""
    return fly(np.random.default_rng([base_seed, k]))


def campaign(base_seed, n):
    """Every case's load, each case seeded independently."""
    return [rerun(base_seed, k) for k in range(n)]


def report(loads, limit):
    """Cases over the limit, the worst case, and a rule-of-three bound if none failed."""
    if not loads:
        raise ValueError("no cases")
    over = [k for k, load in enumerate(loads) if load > limit]
    worst = max(range(len(loads)), key=lambda k: loads[k])
    return {
        "over": over,
        "worst": (worst, round(loads[worst], 2)),
        "bound": None if over else 3 / len(loads),
    }

def trace(requirements, code, tests):
    """Gaps in the requirement-to-code-to-test links."""
    known = set(requirements)
    tested, failing, implemented = set(), set(), set()
    unknown = []

def fly_frames(frames, rule):
    """Actuator commands per frame, and how many frames it held, for three fail-silent strings."""
    if rule not in ("two", "any"):
        raise ValueError(f"unknown rule {rule!r}")
    current = 0.0
    commands = []
    held = 0
    for frame in frames:
        sent = [pair[0] for pair in frame if pair is not None and pair[0] == pair[1]]
        value = None
        if rule == "two":
            for v in sent:
                if sent.count(v) >= 2:
                    value = v
                    break
        elif sent and all(v == sent[0] for v in sent):
            value = sent[0]
        if value is None:
            held += 1
        else:
            current = value
        commands.append(current)
    return (commands, held)

def design_loop(J, wn, zeta, delay_s, margin_deg, bend_hz):
    """PD gains plus the delay and bending checks for one attitude axis."""
    if J <= 0 or wn <= 0 or zeta <= 0:

    }

import math


def max_q(samples, rho0=1.225, H=8500.0):
    """Time and size of peak dynamic pressure, and the run of samples within 10% of it."""
    if not samples:
        raise ValueError("no samples")
    q = [0.5 * rho0 * math.exp(-alt / H) * v**2 for _, alt, v in samples]
    peak = max(range(len(q)), key=lambda i: q[i])
    floor = 0.9 * q[peak]
    lo = peak
    while lo > 0 and q[lo - 1] >= floor:
        lo -= 1
    hi = peak
    while hi < len(q) - 1 and q[hi + 1] >= floor:
        hi += 1
    return {"t": samples[peak][0], "q": int(round(q[peak])), "window": (samples[lo][0], samples[hi][0])}

def check_story(text, wpm=140):
    """Issues with a STAR story written one labelled line per part."""
    lines = [line.strip() for line in text.splitlines() if line.strip()]
    if len(lines) != 4 or any(not line.startswith(label) for line, label in zip(lines, LABELS)):

    return issues

What does this program print?

```cpp
#include <cstdio>

struct Guard {
    const char* name;
    ~Guard() { std::printf("%s ", name); }
};

int main() {
    Guard a{"a"};
    {
        Guard b{"b"};
        Guard c{"c"};
    }
    Guard d{"d"};
}
```

Type the four letters in the order they are printed, separated by spaces.
--- answer
c b d a
cbda
c, b, d, a
c,b,d,a
--- why
A destructor runs automatically when its object goes out of scope, and objects in one scope are destroyed in the reverse order of their construction. At the inner closing brace, `c` goes first and then `b`. At the end of `main`, `d` goes before `a`. This is RAII at work: no line asks for the cleanup, and it happens at predictable points.

+++ question | Counting owners
--- ask
What does this program print?

```cpp
#include <cstdio>
#include <memory>
#include <utility>

int main() {
    auto s = std::make_shared<int>(5);
    auto t = s;
    {
        auto u = t;
        auto v = std::make_unique<int>(7);
        std::printf("%ld ", s.use_count());
    }
    auto w = std::move(t);
    std::printf("%ld\n", s.use_count());
}
```

Type the two numbers, separated by a space.
--- answer
3 2
3, 2
3,2
--- why
Inside the block, `s`, `t` and `u` all own the integer, so the count is 3; the `unique_ptr` `v` has nothing to do with it. At the closing brace `u` is destroyed, bringing the count to 2. Moving `t` into `w` transfers ownership without touching the count: `t` becomes empty and `w` takes its place, so it stays 2. That is why a move of a `shared_ptr` is cheaper than a copy, which would need an atomic increment.

+++ question | An atomic counter that still loses counts
--- ask
Two threads each run this loop, then `main` joins them and prints `frames`.

```cpp
std::atomic<int> frames{0};

void worker() {
    for (int i = 0; i < 100000; ++i)
        frames = frames + 1;
}
```

What can it print?
--- choice
Always 200000, because `frames` is `std::atomic`.
--- choice correct
Sometimes less than 200000: `frames + 1` is an atomic read and `frames = ...` a separate atomic write, so the other thread can write in between and one update is lost. `++frames` or `fetch_add` makes the whole increment one atomic step.
--- choice
Anything at all: it is a data race and therefore undefined behavior, just as with a plain `int`.
--- choice
Nothing: it does not compile, because `std::atomic` has no `+` operator.
--- why
`std::atomic` makes each single operation on the variable indivisible. Here there are two: a load (the conversion inside `frames + 1`) and a store (the assignment). Another thread's whole increment can land between them, and its update is overwritten, the same lost update as the whiteboard tally. Because every access is atomic, it is not a data race and not undefined, just wrong. It compiles because the atomic converts to `int` when read.

+++ question | The crash that a printf cures
--- ask
A telemetry decoder crashes only in the optimized build. When you add a `printf` near the suspect line, the crash goes away. What is the best reading of this, and the next step?
--- choice
The compiler's optimizer has a bug. Build without optimization and ship that.
--- choice
The `printf` fixed a timing problem. Keep it in and move on.
--- choice correct
It is very likely undefined behavior, such as an out-of-bounds read or use after free, whose symptom moves with code layout. Rebuild with `-g -fsanitize=address,undefined`, reproduce, and read the first report.
--- choice
Output buffering is hiding the crash. Print to `stderr` and the bug will show itself.
--- why
A bug that appears and disappears when unrelated code changes is the classic sign of undefined behavior: the compiler assumes it never happens, so what the program actually does depends on layout and optimization. A crash that vanishes without an explanation is still there, hidden. Sanitizers check how the answer was reached and stop at the exact line, which is why they are the next step, not guessing.

+++ question | Sizing a smaller fleet

A fleet of 3000 satellites each sends 1000 channels, two bytes a value, twice a second. Which is closest to the data it produces?

--- choice correct

--- choice
About 10 TB a day, about 4 PB a year
--- choice
About 1 GB a day, about 0.4 TB a year
--- why
3000 times 1000 times 2 bytes times 2 per second is 12,000,000 bytes a second, 12 MB/s. +++ question | What keeps a campaign reproducible
--- ask
Which of these does **not** break bit-for-bit reproducibility of a seeded Monte Carlo campaign?
--- choice correct
Running cases on many cores in whatever order they finish, when each case's generator is seeded from the base seed and its case number and its result goes into its own slot.
--- choice
Drawing every case's random numbers from one shared generator, in the order the threads happen to reach it.
--- choice
Seeding the generator from the wall clock at the start of each night.
--- choice
Adding each case's result into a single floating-point total as soon as the case finishes.
--- why
Determinism needs the same inputs to give identical outputs. A seed derived from the base seed and the case number makes each case independent of timing, and a slot per case keeps the results in case order. A shared generator hands numbers out in thread-timing order, a clock seed changes every night, and summing in finishing order changes the rounding.

+++ question | MC/DC for two pairs
--- ask
How many test cases does a minimal MC/DC test set need for this decision, with four conditions?

$$
\text{fire} = (A \land B) \lor (C \land D)
$$

Type the number.
--- answer
5
five
--- why
For N conditions, MC/DC needs at least N + 1 tests, and for this decision that minimum can be met: 5 tests. One such set is A B C D = 1001, 0101, 1101, 1011 and 1010. 1001 does not fire. Setting A alone from 0101 to 1101 makes it fire, which shows A. From 1001, setting B alone (1101) makes it fire, which shows B, and setting C alone (1011) makes it fire, which shows C. From 1010, which does not fire, setting D alone (1011) makes it fire, which shows D. Five vectors cover all four conditions, against 16 for exhaustive testing.

+++ question | Two strings silent at once
--- ask
In one frame, two of the three strings each see their cores disagree and go silent. The third sends a good command. What does the actuator's judge do?
--- choice
It acts on the command under either rule, because a silent string never lies.
--- choice correct
Under "act on two that agree" it holds its last command; under "act on any command that arrives" it acts on the good one.
--- choice
It holds under either rule, because one string cannot be trusted.
--- choice
It asks the remaining string's two cores to vote again.
--- why
Fail-silent strings never send a wrong command, so one arriving command is trustworthy. A judge that needs two agreeing commands cannot act with only one, so it holds; a judge that acts on any arriving command keeps flying. That is the difference between the two probabilities in the lesson: losing command when two or more strings fail (about 3 in a million in the illustration) versus only when all three fail (about 1 in a billion). It is why you must say which rule you assume.

+++ question | Same gains, heavier vehicle
--- ask
A PD attitude loop was tuned for inertia J. Propellant loading doubles the inertia, and the gains are left unchanged. What happens to the natural frequency and the damping ratio?
--- choice
The natural frequency halves; the damping ratio stays the same.
--- choice correct
Both fall by a factor of the square root of 2, about 1.41: the loop is slower and less damped, so it overshoots more.
--- choice
Neither changes, because the gains are what set the response.
--- choice
The natural frequency falls and the damping ratio rises, so it creeps more slowly.
--- why
From kp = J times wn squared, wn is the square root of kp over J, so doubling J divides it by the square root of 2. From kd = 2 zeta wn J, zeta is kd over 2 times the square root of kp times J, so it also falls by the square root of 2. Lower damping means more overshoot. Gains carry the design's assumptions about the vehicle, which is why they are scheduled with mass properties.
